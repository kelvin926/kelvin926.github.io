  1. AIM
    KALO: Kangaroo-LocoManipulation for Early Launch Acceleration in a Wheel-Legged Robot
    Hyunseo Jang, Seungmin Kim, Donggi Choi, and Youngeun Song
    In IEEE/ASME International Conference on Advanced Intelligent Mechatronics (AIM), Jul 2026
    Published
    Abs DOI HTML

    This paper presents KALO, short for Kangaroo-LocoManipulation, a simulation-based, biomimetically motivated launch controller for improving the early acceleration of a wheel-legged robot. The method repurposes the onboard OpenArm manipulator on a Go2W base as a constrained tail-like inertial appendage during the launch phase and learns a constrained single-joint arm residual from a settled pre-launch state. We evaluate KALO in deterministic simulation rollouts in NVIDIA Isaac Lab, aligned to launch onset, and compare it with a stowed-arm locomotion baseline and a fixed carrying-pose baseline under an early-window protocol focused on the first 1.5 s. Across this transient, KALO accumulates forward velocity faster, reaches target-speed fractions sooner than both baselines, and remains substantially more directionally stable than the fixed carrying-pose controller. The results indicate that, in simulation, the onboard manipulator can function as a short-horizon launch-assist appendage in addition to its manipulation role.

  2. AIM
    A Lift-Equipped Mobile Manipulator Platform for Shelf Transfer in Multi-Level Rack Environments
    Jiseung Ha, Hyunseo Jang, Donggi Choi, and Youngeun Song
    In IEEE/ASME International Conference on Advanced Intelligent Mechatronics (AIM), 2026
    Published
    Abs

    This paper presents a lift-equipped mobile manipulator platform and a reinforcement learning environment for shelf transfer in a multi-level rack. The task requires grasping an object from a source shelf, extracting it from the shelf, transporting it to a target shelf location, releasing it, and completing final placement. Compared with tabletop pick-and-place, the setting introduces constrained shelf geometry, vertical reach requirements, and coordinated lift-arm motion. We formulate the task in Isaac Lab using a fixed-base lift-enabled AMR-arm platform with a right-arm manipulation setup, a 9-D action space, a 41-D state observation, staged reward terms, and a placement-oriented task completion criterion. Policies are trained with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and the evaluation protocol measures end-to-end success together with intermediate extraction and placement metrics. The proposed platform is intended to support the study of vertically structured warehouse manipulation in which source-shelf extraction, inter-level transport, and target-shelf placement must all be achieved within a single episode.

  3. CASE
    Design and Implementation of a Local Delivery Robot System: A Reinforcement Learning Approach
    Seungmin Kim, Aoyu Xie, Hyunseo Jang, Joohee Kim, Donggi Choi, and Youngeun Song
    In IEEE International Conference on Automation Science and Engineering (CASE), 2026
    Accepted
    Abs

    This paper presents a dual-handler robotic system for automated parcel storage in local logistics environments, such as office complexes and residential communities. The system employs a Dueling Double Deep Q-Network (D3QN) to learn optimal storage allocation strategies for incoming parcels of varying dimensions. To improve training efficiency, we reduce the action space from ten individual movement commands to a binary handler-selection scheme, achieving a 90 percent reduction in required operational steps while maintaining full functionality. The observation space incorporates storage layout status, parcel dimensions, handler states, and rack occupancy to enable context-aware decision-making. Simulation results demonstrate stable learning convergence and effective dual-handler coordination. These results provide a practical foundation for deploying reinforcement learning-based automation in local-scale logistics operations.

  4. AEI
    Clustering-enhanced Bayesian optimization (CEBO): A case study on T-pedal wheel design of a stair-climbing robot
    Hyunseo Jang, Hyeonbeen Seo, Youngeun Song, and Ungki Lee
    Advanced Engineering Informatics, 2026
    Abs DOI

    Summary of the published article: CEBO uses a clustering-based trust region (CBTR) to guide Bayesian optimization. Surrogate predictions screen candidate points, which are grouped using K-means with silhouette-based cluster selection. Information from the selected cluster defines a search region, where expected improvement selects the next evaluation. This approach combines information from multiple candidates instead of centering the search solely on one incumbent solution. The study evaluates the method on benchmark functions and a T-pedal wheel design problem for a stair-climbing robot.

  1. Electronics
    Efficient Graph-Based Multi-Story Path Planning with Optimized Elevator Selection for Indoor Delivery Robots
    Seungmin Kim, Hyunseo Jang, Jiseung Ha, Daekug Lee, Yeongho Ha, and Youngeun Song
    Electronics, 2025
    Abs DOI

    Multi-story path planning (MSPP) for indoor delivery robots presents unique challenges in modern logistics systems, particularly in efficiently handling multiple deliveries across different floors. This paper proposes a graph-based path planning algorithm that optimizes delivery routes in multi-story buildings while maintaining computational efficiency. The algorithm, with a complexity of O(|E| log |V|), integrates three key components: (1) a floor-based parcel grouping strategy, (2) an optimized Dijkstra-based path planning system, and (3) an efficient elevator selection mechanism. The modular design allows for adaptability to diverse building layouts and logistics scenarios. Evaluated in a 15-story simulation with varying parcel counts (n = 1 to 7), the algorithm demonstrated minimal runtime increase (1.28 s to 1.35 s) while ensuring optimal path selection. Delivery distances ranged from 48 m for a single parcel to 126 m for seven parcels, demonstrating the system’s scalability and efficiency in complex multi-story environments. Unlike traditional methods such as A*, the proposed approach guarantees optimality while effectively addressing vertical navigation challenges. These results validate its practical applicability in smart building environments and modern logistics systems, offering a robust solution for real-world delivery challenges.

  2. Sensors
    Time-Interval-Based Collision Detection for 4WIS Mobile Robots in Human-Shared Indoor Environments
    Seungmin Kim, Hyunseo Jang, Jiseung Ha, Daekug Lee, Yeongho Ha, and Youngeun Song
    Sensors, 2025
    Abs DOI

    The recent growth in e-commerce has significantly increased the demand for indoor delivery solutions, highlighting challenges in last-mile delivery. This study presents a time-interval-based collision detection method for Four-Wheel Independent Steering (4WIS) mobile robots operating in human-shared indoor environments, where traditional path following algorithms often create unpredictable movements. By integrating kinematic-based robot trajectory calculation with LiDAR-based human detection and Kalman filter-based prediction, our system enables more natural robot-human interactions. Experimental results demonstrate that our parallel driving mode achieves superior human detection performance compared to conventional Ackermann steering, particularly during cornering and high-speed operations. The proposed method’s effectiveness is validated through comprehensive experiments in realistic indoor scenarios, showing its potential for improving the efficiency and safety of indoor autonomous navigation systems.
